# Survey on Video and Multimodal Generation

## TL;DR  
- Video generation techniques have evolved through the integration of diffusion models and generative networks, facilitating breakthroughs in quality and controllability [1][2].  
- Multimodal generation leverages advances in unified learning models, allowing for flexible generation across various output types, enhancing user interactivity [3][4].  
- The application of AI in video generation is transforming content creation, democratizing access, and leading to varied applications from social media to education [5][6].  
- Key challenges remain in achieving efficient multimodal integration and developing comprehensive evaluation methods across diverse modalities [7][8].

## Background  
Video generation pertains to the creation of visual media using algorithms that can interpret and replicate motion and storytelling. This technology is crucial now due to its rapid adoption in industries such as entertainment, marketing, and education. Foundational works include frameworks that combine various generative models, particularly differentiating between image and video synthesis challenges to enhance video quality and align generation with user input [1][9]. Recent advancements, particularly through diffusion models, have significantly improved video realism and tractability, illustrating the merging of traditional techniques with modern methodologies [2][10].

## Foundations of Video Generation  
Research has established critical frameworks and iterative developments in video generation, focusing on hybrid models like VAE-GAN that address previous limitations in diverse video creation. For instance, advancements such as TC-Bench have provided benchmarks that reflect the compositional capabilities of generative models under temporal scenarios [11]. Notably, controllable video generation has emerged as a vital area, where non-textual conditions enhance video synthesis by adding dynamics like camera angles and pacing [9][12].  

## Technologies and Methods in Multimodal Generation  
Recent developments in technologies for multimodal generation reflect heightened integration of different input and output formats. Models like CoDi show promising results in generating videos from multiple inputs [4]. The emphasis on architectures that utilize transformer models aids in achieving seamless interactivity between inputs such as audio, text, and imagery, thus creating coherent multimedia outputs [3][13]. Innovations like EasyGen have aimed to merge LLMs with diffusion models, showcasing improved efficiency in understanding and generating diverse media types [7].

## Applications and Impact of Video Generation  
Video generation technologies are reshaping how content is created across various sectors. For instance, AI-powered tools automate video production, making it accessible to non-professionals in social media and marketing [5]. Furthermore, solutions like Vidmento allow creators to leverage generative media to enhance storytelling abilities, filling narrative gaps and improving audience engagement [6]. The impact extends beyond traditional media, with educational tools benefiting from instructional videos that enhance engagement and interactivity in learning that enhances learning experiences [5].

## Trends and Challenges in Multimodal Generation  
Despite advancements, challenges persist in multimodal generation, particularly regarding computational efficiency and integration robustness. Frameworks like FlowInOne propose methods to unify processes despite these limitations, striving for cohesive data representations across different formats [7]. Moreover, ongoing discussions around model interpretability and comprehensive evaluation methodologies indicate a need for structured approaches to address these unresolved issues in multimodal generation [8][14].  

## Trends and Open Problems  
The last two years have seen significant strides in both video and multimodal generation, yet unresolved questions remain about efficiency and the integration of disparate data types. The evolution of multimodal question answering shows the depth of these challenges, highlighting gaps in cross-modal interactions [15]. Future directions must focus on streamlining generative methodologies while enhancing the performance of integrated models without sacrificing interpretability [8][14].
